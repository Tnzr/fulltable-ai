The short answer

Yes. Free public data is enough to build a good first version, and this prototype is that first version. It pulls six public sources (no paid APIs and no API keys) and scores {{N_TR}} Miami-Dade census tracts. It ranks {{N_IRS}} geocoded nonprofits and associations as people to contact, and {{N_REST}} licensed restaurants with 40+ seats as people to sell to.

The strongest finding is Florida's state restaurant licence file. It lists every licensed restaurant with its address, phone number and number of seats, and it updates regularly. That gives FullTable a ready-made, legal sales list and an exact measure of restaurant capacity on the map.

What each layer means
FullTable opportunity
Demand × √(seat supply). High where many groups gather and there are restaurants to sell to. This is the main targeting layer.
Group-dining demand
Weighted percentile, all counted within 1.5 km of the tract centre: jobs 25%, organizations 30%, residents 15%, hotels 10%, median income 10%, upcoming events 10%.
Restaurant seat supply
Licensed seats within 1.5 km (60%) plus food-service jobs (40%).
Demand minus supply
Gaps: groups exist but seats are scarce. Useful for pitching restaurant groups on expansion, less useful for selling to single restaurants.
Upcoming events (sample)
The 36 sample events from the app's Network Map data. In production this layer reads the live upcomingEvent registry.

Who to contact: organizations ranked by location and fit

Score = 40% type fit (social clubs, business leagues and fraternal or veterans groups eat out together often; youth groups rarely do) + 35% opportunity score of the organization's tract + 25% size (reported income, as a percentile). Addresses were matched to map points with the free Census batch geocoder. 88% matched; the unmatched ones are mostly PO boxes.

How it plugs into the lead engine

Each organization becomes an organizer candidate with verificationStatus=UNVERIFIED, the source name "IRS EO BMF" and its EIN as a stable ID, so duplicates merge. Then the existing leadEngineScrapeEventSourceV2 crawls its website to find events and a business contact route. A person approves it before any outreach.

Who to sell to: restaurants with 40+ seats near the most groups

From the state licence file (licence type SEAT, Miami-Dade, 40+ seats; hotels, clubs, caterers and cafeterias removed). Score = 45% demand in the restaurant's tract + 35% organizations per competing 40+ seat restaurant + 20% size fit (40–250 seats). The file also includes a phone number for each restaurant.

How it would run inside the engine

1 · Get the dataWeekly job downloads DBPR, IRS BMF, LODES and ACS files; nightly job calls the Ticketmaster API. Raw files stored with their download date.
2 · Clean and geocodeCensus batch geocoder, with Nominatim/Overture as fallback; IDs are EIN, licence number or GEOID; duplicates merged.
3 · Score areasTract or H3 hexagon scores (demand, supply, opportunity) saved by date, so changes can be tracked over time.
4 · Rank candidatesOrganizations become organizer candidates and restaurants become sales leads, each with a score, the reason for it and the source.
5 · Review and actNetwork Map layer toggles; a person approves each candidate; then the lead engine scrape finds events and contacts.
Decisions for you. (1) Unit of area: census tracts (works with demographic data) or H3 hexagons (even sizes, easier for heat maps). (2) Whether the restaurant list feeds FullTable's own sales pipeline, the restaurant-facing product, or both. (3) Whether to get a free Census API key and a Ticketmaster key now.
Caveats. The weights are my judgment, not fitted to results. Once there is pilot data, fit them to actual group bookings. IRS income measures how big an organization is, not how often it eats out. The LODES jobs data lags about two years. Sample events are the app's demo data, not real events. OSM has few restaurants in Miami, so restaurant supply uses DBPR seats instead. Licence data is public record, but cold outreach still has to follow the CAN-SPAM and TCPA rules.
